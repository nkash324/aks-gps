Purdue GPS Kart Benchmark

Two Pure Pursuit drivers lapping the RTK-surveyed Purdue track in the new 2D kart sim, seeing only what the real sensors would give them: RTK GNSS, IMU and the wheel encoder, fused by one EKF. Driver C is B with a learned neural-network correction on top. Each cell is 30 standing-start laps with a different randomised kart and sensor errors, the same 30 for every driver.

Where the margin goes

The map is assumed calibrated at the track (a few known points measured with the kart's GPS), leaving at most ±5 cm of map offset plus the GNSS error itself. On that basis B finishes every lap at realistic noise, but its closest tyre came within about 2 cm of an edge. Pure Pursuit cuts corners by about 0.2 m of the 0.3 m buffer even with perfect sensors. At harsh noise the RTK float episodes (up to 0.6 m off) push it over 4 times in 30. The second toggle below shows what happens if the map is off by up to 0.3 m.

Driver C · B plus learned corrections

C drives exactly like B, with a small neural network adding a correction 20 times a second: up to ±1 m/s on B's target speed and ±0.02 1/m on its steering curvature, with no added speed during a GNSS outage. It sees only what B sees (the EKF estimate, the map and the GNSS status). Each row is a training checkpoint, run on the same 30 laps per sensor level as A and B with no exploration noise. These checkpoints trained for up to 1M steps (about 1,000 laps) on realistic sensors only, so the perfect and harsh columns test laps they never trained on. "Avg correction" is the mean size of the correction as a share of its maximum.

  • Phase 1 makes C a little faster than B, not yet safer. At realistic noise the 1M checkpoint is 0.46 s quicker but went off on 1 lap in 30. The 250k checkpoint is 0.21 s quicker, with all 30 laps and more room to the edge than B.
  • The gain carries over to sensor levels C never trained on. With perfect sensors the 1M checkpoint is 0.41 s quicker than B and keeps more room to the edge (0.14 m vs 0.10 m). At harsh noise most checkpoints finish 27/30 against B's 26/30.
  • Harsh noise is still the weak spot. RTK float episodes push C off just as they push B. Training phase 2 mixes in harsh laps (up to 40%) to teach it to back off when the fix is lost.
  • Progress isn't steady between checkpoints. 750k is no faster than B, while 1M is the fastest. Phase 2 saves checkpoints more often and ranks them on separate laps, then benchmarks the top three here.

What's in the sim

  • Kart: four tyres with load-dependent grip and combined slip, weight transfer, solid rear axle that scrubs in corners, steering jacking, motor and battery-limited regen, hydraulic rear brake held at the bite point, steering delay and rate limit.
  • Randomised per run: grip ±15%, mass, motor force, regen power, brake force and timing, steering rate and delay, jacking.
  • Sensors: RTK at 20 Hz with 50 ms delay, noise, wander, float episodes and dropouts; IMU with bias; wheel encoder with scale error. Map calibrated at the track to within ±0.05 m.
  • Off track: any part of any tyre past a surveyed edge. The pit-lane openings count as edges.
  • Drivers: Pure Pursuit, look-ahead 0.35 × speed + 1 m (2–6 m), tuned once on separate seeds. Speed plans: 0.6 g cornering, 4 m/s² braking, 11.5 m/s cap. Both slow down on RTK float or GNSS loss.
